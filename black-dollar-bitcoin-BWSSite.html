Start here: how to use this tool

This tool answers one question: if Black Americans set aside a small, steady amount of their spending for Bitcoin, what could it grow into? You don't need to know anything about Bitcoin to use it. Give it two minutes, then play with the numbers.

Buying power

The money Black households have left after taxes to spend. It is estimated at close to $2 trillion a year. It measures spending ability, not savings sitting in a bank.

Dollar-cost averaging

Putting in the same amount on a regular schedule, no matter the price. When the price is low your money buys more Bitcoin, and when it's high it buys less. You never have to guess the best day to buy.

Price swings

Bitcoin's price goes up and down, sometimes by a lot. The tool shows the rough patches as well as the wins, so you can see what holding through them would have felt like.

Five steps

  1. Look at where the money goes.Pick a spending year under “Set your scenario.” The page shows the biggest spending categories and how spending rises and falls through the year.
  2. Choose your years.Pick the year to start buying Bitcoin and the year to stop. Try a few. Starting at a different time can change the result a lot.
  3. Say how many people take part.The people box is the size of the community in millions. The slider is the share of those people who actually join. Not everyone would, so try 5% to 10% for a realistic picture. At 100%, the community would own a huge slice of all Bitcoin.
  4. Set how often and how much.Choose daily, weekly, or monthly deposits. Then enter a dollar amount each person puts in, or switch to a percent of their spending. Small amounts add up when many people do it together.
  5. Read the results.Scroll down to see what was put in, what it's worth, and how it moved over time. Then check the comparison tables to see how the start year and the amount change the outcome.

What the result cards mean

Total put inEvery dollar the community deposited during your window. This is the money out of pocket.
Bitcoin boughtHow much Bitcoin those deposits bought at each day's price, and what share of all Bitcoin that is.
Worth at the end of the windowWhat that Bitcoin was worth on the last day you picked. The percent is the gain or loss on what was put in.
Lowest pointThe worst moment along the way, shown as how much the portfolio was worth compared with what had been put in. This is the honest picture of the ride.

Three things to try first

  • Start in 2018, deposit $20 a month, and set the participation slider to 10%. Note the lowest point, then the final value.
  • Switch from monthly to daily and compare the percent gain. It barely moves, because the buy days change but the price path stays the same.
  • Open the start-year table in “Compare the options.” See how a different start year changes the multiple.

It's already happening in Chicago

The Altgeld Bitcoin Reserve was founded in Altgeld Gardens, a public housing community on Chicago's South Side. It describes itself as the first community-operated Bitcoin treasury in the United States, held in a 2-of-3 multisig cold storage vault, with holdings anyone can check on-chain. The Illinois House of Representatives recognized it as the nation's first community-run Bitcoin reserve in October 2025.

It matters as a precedent. A reserve that started in public housing now appears on BitcoinTreasuries.net, the tracker that also lists companies like Strategy and Tesla. At last check it ranked #69 among private entities. The amount is small, but a neighborhood that banks overlooked is on the same public ledger as Wall Street firms.

Read this before you share these numbers

  • This is education, not financial advice. It shows what would have happened in the past. Bitcoin can fall more than 70% from a peak, and it has. The “lowest point” number above shows how rough the ride was for the window you picked.
  • Buying power is not savings or wealth. It is an estimate of after-tax income available to spend, and it is debated by economists. Most households would not set aside money the way this model assumes, which is why the participation slider exists.
  • Category shares are modeled. The split follows the general pattern of the Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Expenditure Survey for Black households. Month-to-month swings (the holiday bump, back-to-school) are illustrative, not measured.
  • Bitcoin prices are daily closing prices pulled live from CryptoCompare when your browser is online. Daily deposits buy at each day's close, weekly deposits buy on Mondays, and monthly deposits buy on the 1st. If the live feed can't load, the page falls back to rounded month-end prices connected day to day, so daily and weekly results are only rough estimates. It says so at the top.
  • Price impact is ignored. The model buys at the historical monthly price as if the community's buying never moved the market. With a large group putting in even small amounts, the real market would likely have moved against them. Check the “share of all Bitcoin” number in the Bitcoin bought card. Lower the participation slider to see a more realistic scale.
  • Fees, taxes, and the cost of buying are not included.
